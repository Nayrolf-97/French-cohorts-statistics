Reconstructed from official French death records (INSEE), deduplicated. Pick a sex, a measure, and any set of birth cohorts.

Dashed segments are not final. The figures come from recorded deaths, so a value is only settled once no further death can be added to it. For the cumulative measure this means the whole curve of a living cohort is provisional — any surviving member will eventually be counted at every age. For deaths at an exact age, only the ages people can still die at remain open. Cohorts up to 1916 (men) and 1911 (women) are treated as extinct.

Reading the young ages. For cohorts that are still alive, the numbers count people already dead, not people alive — at ages far below the cohort's current age the curve reflects how many have died so far, which is only a small part of the generation. Solid lines (extinct cohorts) are the ones to compare directly.
